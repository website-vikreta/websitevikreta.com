# SEO / AEO Research — AI Automations (2026-11-07)

Method and limits. WebSearch returns a summarised result list (titles, URLs, a synthesis). It does NOT expose People Also Ask boxes, featured snippets, AI Overview text or rank positions. So: "ranks" below means "surfaced for the query", not a verified position, and FAQ questions are marked **[suggested]** (derived from query phrasing, not scraped PAA). Verify PAA/AIO manually in a browser (or a SERP tool) before final copy. Vendor claims are marked unverified. No scores. 12 queries run (8 branch-level, 4 compliance). Branch coverage is one query each; a second pass per branch is recommended.

## A. Cross-cutting findings

1. Almost everything that surfaces is vendor marketing. A page that states plain, checkable facts (what it does, what it needs, limits, legal) is rare, so it is the gap to fill.
2. Winning formats seen: vendor guide posts ("ultimate guide 2025/2026"), vendor help docs (QuickBooks, Meta), law-firm explainers (FCC/TCPA, NYC LL144), listicles/directories (DesignRush, Clutch, "top X agencies"), pricing-range posts (AI automation cost), Fiverr gigs. Not seen: honest "what is NOT included", real timelines, named-client case studies with numbers.
3. Price ranges are what people want and what AI answers quote (see workflow row). We do not show prices (locked decision), so substitute with scope, time-to-live, and "what drives cost" content.
4. Compliance pages by law firms are the quoted sources for legal questions. Link out to primary sources (FCC, FTC, DCWP, Meta) rather than restating loosely.
5. Freshness matters: pages carry "2025/2026" and "as of July 2026" in titles. Add visible "Last reviewed" dates.
